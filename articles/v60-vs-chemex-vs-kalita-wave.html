    V60 vs Chemex vs Kalita Wave: Which Dripper Should You Buy First?

    By Groundwork Coffee Team · September 19, 2026 · 9 min read

    The three classic drippers get compared on specs everywhere and on taste nowhere. So we fixed that: same beans (a washed Ethiopian, roasted 12 days prior), same 20g-to-320g ratio, same 200°F water, three drippers — then we tasted blind, no labels, and wrote down what each cup actually tasted like. Here's the honest breakdown, including the cost math nobody shows you.

    Blind Taste Test: What Each Cup Actually Tasted Like

    Three tasters, three unmarked cups, one Ethiopian with apricot and jasmine notes. The results were more decisive than we expected:

    • V60: the brightest and most expressive — apricot up front, floral finish, lightest body. When the pour was controlled, it was the clear winner. When the pour wobbled (we tested a deliberately sloppy pour too), it dropped to last: thin and sharp.
    • Chemex: the cleanest cup by a mile. The thick filter stripped out nearly all oils and fines, leaving a tea-like, sparkling cup. Slightly less intense flavor than the V60 at its best — but zero sediment and the most "refined" tasting of the three.
    • Kalita Wave: the most balanced and the most consistent. Good sweetness, medium body, all three tasters' cups tasted nearly identical across two brews. It never reached the V60's highs, but it never produced a bad cup either.

    The takeaway: the V60 has the highest ceiling and the lowest floor. The Kalita has the highest floor. The Chemex plays a different game entirely — clarity over intensity.

    Forgiveness Ranking: Which Punishes Bad Technique Most

    1. Kalita Wave — most forgiving. The flat bed and three small drain holes regulate drawdown for you. Pour too fast? The bed depth limits the damage. Uneven spiral? The flat bed evens it out. This is the dripper for learning.
    2. Chemex — moderately forgiving. The thick filter slows everything down, which masks small pouring errors — but the large brew size tempts people into coarse grinds and under-extraction.
    3. V60 — least forgiving. Total control means total responsibility. A fast pour channels, a slow pour stalls, and the cup tells on you every time. Rewarding once dialed; frustrating for the first month.

    Cost of Ownership: Filter Prices Add Up

    This is the math that changes decisions. The brewer is a one-time purchase; filters are forever. At one brew per day for a year (365 filters):

    • V60: 365 × ~$0.12 ≈ $44/year
    • Kalita Wave: 365 × ~$0.25 ≈ $91/year
    • Chemex: 365 × ~$0.48 ≈ $175/year

    The Chemex costs roughly four times the V60 in filters alone — over three years, that's a $390 difference, dwarfing the brewer price gap. If you brew daily, factor this in. (Buying filters in 200-count packs and catching subscribe-and-save deals cuts 15–25% off these numbers.)

    Buy the V60 If… / Buy the Chemex If… / Buy the Kalita Wave If…

    Buy the V60 if you want the brightest, most expressive cups and you're willing to spend a month learning pour control. It's $10–$25, the filters are cheapest, and at its best nothing under $50 beats it. Start here if you already brew and want to level up.

    Buy the Chemex if you hate any sediment or muddiness and love a clean, tea-like cup — or you regularly brew for 2–3 people (the 6-cup size is genuinely useful). Accept the filter cost as the price of that clarity. It's also the best-looking brewer ever made, which counts on a counter.

    Buy the Kalita Wave if this is your first dripper. Full stop. You'll get good, consistent cups in week one instead of month two, and consistency is what keeps beginners brewing. The 155 size (1–2 cups) suits solo brewers; the 185 (2–4 cups) suits households.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    Can one grinder serve all three drippers?

    Yes. All three brew in the medium to medium-fine range — roughly kosher salt to fine sand. The Chemex wants the coarsest of the three, the V60 the finest, but any decent burr grinder covers the whole spread. See our grinder guide for picks.

    Which is easiest to clean?

    The V60 — rinse the cone and toss the filter. The Chemex's narrow neck needs a long brush for deep cleaning, and the Kalita's three holes can trap grounds if you don't rinse right away. All three take under a minute with a prompt rinse.

    Plastic, ceramic, glass, or metal dripper?

    For the V60: plastic. It's cheap, nearly unbreakable, and holds heat as well as ceramic in practice (plastic insulates; ceramic absorbs heat from the slurry). For the Kalita: stainless steel if you can find it, ceramic otherwise. The Chemex only comes in glass — that's the point.
